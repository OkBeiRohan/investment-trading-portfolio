## 📖 Overview

The National Pension System (NPS) is a government-backed, low-cost retirement savings scheme designed to provide financial security post-retirement. This module explains NPS architecture (Tier I mandatory, Tier II voluntary), investment choices (equity, corporate bonds, government securities, alternative assets), fund manager selection, and exit/withdrawal rules.

You'll learn why NPS offers **triple tax benefits** (deductions under 80C, 80CCD(1B), 80CCD(2)), how it compares to EPF/PPF/mutual funds, and the trade-offs (lock-in till 60, mandatory annuity purchase). The module covers Tier I vs Tier II accounts, auto-choice vs active choice asset allocation, partial withdrawals, and systematic lump-sum withdrawal (SLW).

Special topics include NPS Vatsalya (for minors), Corporate NPS (employer contributions), and exit strategies (lump sum + annuity combinations). By the end, you'll know if NPS fits your retirement plan and how to maximize tax savings while optimizing returns.

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Tier I vs Tier II Accounts

| Feature                        | Tier I (Mandatory)                  | Tier II (Voluntary)                             |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Lock-in**                    | Till age 60 (retirement)            | No lock-in, withdraw anytime                    |
| **Minimum Contribution**       | ₹1,000/year (₹500/contribution)     | ₹250/contribution                               |
| **Tax Benefit (Contribution)** | ✅ 80C (₹1.5L) + 80CCD(1B) (₹50K)   | ✅ Limited (Tier II for govt employees only)    |
| **Tax on Withdrawal**          | 60% tax-free, 40% annuity (taxable) | Taxable as per slab                             |
| **Mandatory**                  | Yes (if opening NPS)                | Optional (needs Tier I first)                   |
| **Purpose**                    | Retirement corpus                   | Short-term savings (emergency fund alternative) |

**Recommendation:** Focus on Tier I for retirement (tax benefits), use Tier II only if you need quasi-liquid savings.

### Asset Classes in NPS

| Asset Class                     | Code | Allocation                   | Risk        | Returns (Historical)   |
| ------------------------------- | ---- | ---------------------------- | ----------- | ---------------------- |
| **Equity (E)**                  | E    | Max 75% (<50Y), 50% (50-60Y) | High        | 10-12%                 |
| **Corporate Bonds (C)**         | C    | No limit                     | Medium      | 8-9%                   |
| **Government Securities (G)**   | G    | No limit                     | Low         | 7-8%                   |
| **Alternative Investments (A)** | A    | Max 5%                       | Medium-High | Varies (REITs, InvITs) |

**Auto-Choice (Lifecycle-based):**

```
Age <35: 75% E, 15% C, 10% G (aggressive)
Age 35-45: 60% E, 25% C, 15% G (moderate)
Age 45-55: 40% E, 35% C, 25% G (conservative)
Age 55-60: 15% E, 45% C, 40% G (ultra-conservative)

Automatic rebalancing as you age (no action required)
```

**Active Choice (DIY Allocation):**

- Choose your own mix (e.g., 50% E, 30% C, 20% G)
- Rebalance manually (max 2 asset shifts/year)
- Switch fund managers if dissatisfied

### Triple Tax Benefit (EEE for Tier I)

**Tax Deductions (Contribution Phase):**

```
80C: ₹1,50,000 (EPF, PPF, life insurance, ELSS, NPS Tier I)
80CCD(1B): ₹50,000 (NPS Tier I only - additional)
80CCD(2): Unlimited (10% of salary - Corporate NPS employer contribution)

Example (Salaried Employee):
- Salary: ₹12L/year
- 80C: ₹1.5L (EPF ₹1L + NPS Tier I ₹50K)
- 80CCD(1B): ₹50K (additional NPS Tier I)
- 80CCD(2): ₹1.2L (10% employer contribution)

Total Tax-Free Contribution: ₹2.7L
Tax Saved (30% bracket): ₹81,000/year

Over 30 years: ₹24.3L tax saved!
```

**Tax on Withdrawal (Age 60):**

- 60% lump sum: **Tax-free**
- 40% annuity purchase: Annuity income **taxable as per slab**

**Partial EET (Exempt-Exempt-Taxable):** 40% annuity is taxed, not EEE like PPF.

### Exit Rules (Age 60 - Normal Retirement)

```
Total Corpus @ 60: ₹1 Cr (example)

Option 1 (Minimum Compliance):
- 40% annuity (mandatory): ₹40L → Buy pension plan (₹25,000/month taxable income)
- 60% lump sum (tax-free): ₹60L → Take home

Option 2 (Defer annuity):
- 100% lump sum if annuity purchased >₹2L (from 40% of corpus)
- If ₹40L annuity buys >₹2L (i.e., corpus >₹5L), can take 100% lump sum

Option 3 (Full annuity):
- 100% annuity: ₹1 Cr → ₹62,500/month pension (6.25% annuity rate)
- Higher monthly income, zero lump sum
```

**Partial Withdrawal (Pre-Retirement):**

- **25% of contributions** (max 3 times during NPS tenure)
- Allowed for: Child education, marriage, medical emergency, house purchase
- Example: Contributed ₹20L over 15 years → Withdraw ₹5L (25%) for child's education

## 💡 Practical Applications

### For Salaried Employees

**Scenario: Age 30, ₹10L salary, 30 years to retirement**

**Strategy:**

1. **Tier I Contribution:** ₹2L/year (₹1.5L under 80C + ₹50K under 80CCD(1B))
2. **Asset Allocation:** Auto-choice (aggressive - 75% equity)
3. **Corporate NPS:** Request employer to contribute ₹1L (10% of basic) - 80CCD(2)
4. **Total Annual Investment:** ₹3L (₹2L self + ₹1L employer)

**Projected Corpus @ 60 (assuming 10% CAGR):**

```
Total Contributions: ₹90L (₹3L × 30 years)
Final Corpus: ₹5.4 Cr

Exit Plan:
- 60% lump sum (tax-free): ₹3.24 Cr
- 40% annuity (₹2.16 Cr → ₹1.35L/month pension @ 6.25% rate)
```

**Tax Saved Over 30 Years:**

- Self contribution (₹2L × 30% × 30Y): ₹18L
- Employer contribution (₹1L × 30% × 30Y): ₹9L
- **Total tax savings: ₹27L**

### For Self-Employed

**Scenario: Consultant, age 35, irregular income**

**Strategy:**

1. **Minimum Tier I:** ₹1,000/year (maintain account active)
2. **Flexible contributions:** Contribute ₹2L in good income years (80C + 80CCD(1B) benefit)
3. **No employer contribution:** Miss out on 80CCD(2) (only for salaried)

**Alternative:** Consider PPF (₹1.5L/year, EEE tax treatment) if NPS lock-in is restrictive.

## 📊 Quick Reference

### NPS vs Other Retirement Plans

| Feature                  | NPS Tier I          | EPF                  | PPF                 | Mutual Funds (ELSS)   |
| ------------------------ | ------------------- | -------------------- | ------------------- | --------------------- |
| **Lock-in**              | Till 60             | Till 58 (retirement) | 15 years            | 3 years               |
| **Tax Benefit**          | ₹2L (80C + 1B)      | ₹1.5L (80C)          | ₹1.5L (80C)         | ₹1.5L (80C)           |
| **Returns (Historical)** | 10-12% (equity mix) | 8.15% (fixed)        | 7.1% (fixed)        | 12-15% (equity)       |
| **Flexibility**          | Auto/Active choice  | None (fixed)         | None (fixed)        | High (fund selection) |
| **Exit Taxation**        | 60% tax-free        | 100% tax-free (EEE)  | 100% tax-free (EEE) | LTCG 10% (>₹1L)       |
| **Cost**                 | 0.02-0.1%           | Nil                  | Nil                 | 0.5-2.5%              |
| **Annuity**              | 40% mandatory       | Optional             | None                | None                  |

**Winner (Tax + Returns):** NPS (highest deduction ₹2L, equity exposure, low cost)  
**Winner (Simplicity + Tax-free):** PPF (EEE, no annuity hassle)

| PFM                               | Equity (E) 5Y Return | Corporate Bonds (C) 5Y Return | Govt Bonds (G) 5Y Return |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **SBI Pension**                   | 10.5%                | 8.3%                          | 7.8%                     |
| **HDFC Pension**                  | 11.2%                | 8.5%                          | 7.9%                     |
| **ICICI Pru Pension**             | 10.8%                | 8.4%                          | 7.7%                     |
| **LIC Pension**                   | 9.8%                 | 8.1%                          | 7.6%                     |
| **UTI Retirement**                | 10.3%                | 8.2%                          | 7.8%                     |
| **Kotak Pension**                 | 11.0%                | 8.6%                          | 8.0%                     |
| **Aditya Birla Sun Life Pension** | 10.7%                | 8.3%                          | 7.9%                     |

**Note:** Returns vary annually, check latest on [NPSTRUST.org.in](https://npstrust.org.in/)  
**Switching:** Max 2 PFM switches/year (no charges)

### NPS Charges (Ultra-Low Cost)

| Fee Type                | Amount              | Frequency              |
| ----------------------- | ------------------- | ---------------------- |
| **Fund Management**     | 0.02-0.1% of AUM    | Annual                 |
| **Account Maintenance** | ₹0-75/year          | Annual (varies by PFM) |
| **Transaction Fee**     | ₹20-50/contribution | Per transaction        |
| **Exit Fee**            | ₹0                  | Nil                    |

**Example (₹20L corpus):**

- Fund management: ₹2,000/year (0.1%)
- Maintenance: ₹50/year
- **Total cost: ₹2,050/year (0.1% of corpus)**

**vs Mutual Fund (1.5% expense ratio):**

- ₹20L × 1.5% = ₹30,000/year (15x higher!)

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. **Ignoring 80CCD(1B):** Missing ₹50K additional deduction (not using full ₹2L potential)
2. **Not asking employer for Corporate NPS:** Leaving unlimited 80CCD(2) benefit on table
3. **Choosing active allocation without knowledge:** Random asset mix (underperforms auto-choice)
4. **Withdrawing from Tier I prematurely:** Only 3 partial withdrawals allowed (save for real emergencies)
5. **Comparing NPS with PPF on tax alone:** PPF is EEE, NPS is EET (40% annuity taxed)
6. **Opening Tier II without Tier I:** Not eligible (Tier I is prerequisite)
7. **Not switching poor-performing PFMs:** Stuck with LIC Pension (9.8% equity) vs HDFC (11.2%)
8. **Expecting 100% lump sum @ 60:** 40% annuity is mandatory (unless corpus <₹5L)
9. **Not planning annuity purchase:** Rushed decision @ 60 (compare 5+ annuity providers)
10. **Forgetting NPS Vatsalya for kids:** Starting late (age 18) vs early (age 5) - 13 years compounding lost
