## 📖 Overview

Unlike traditional insurance guides, this module approaches insurance through the lens of **behavioral economics** - understanding how cognitive biases, incentives, and framing affect our insurance decisions. You'll learn concepts like perverse incentives (mis-selling), nudges (default options), skin in the game (deductibles), and the Dunning-Kruger effect (overestimating knowledge).

The module dissects common insurance products (term life, health, ULIPs, endowment plans) to reveal hidden costs, conflicts of interest, and optimal choices. You'll understand why commission-based selling leads to bad products, how "free" add-ons aren't free, and which insurance is truly necessary vs marketed.

This isn't about learning policy jargon - it's about thinking clearly when faced with insurance salespeople, advertisements, and relatives pushing "investment-cum-insurance" schemes. By the end, you'll know the golden rule: **Buy term insurance + invest separately (never mix insurance + investment).**

## 🔑 Key Concepts

### Perverse Incentives in Insurance

| Product               | Agent Commission  | Why Pushed                              | What You Should Buy           |
| --------------------- | ----------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Term Insurance**    | 5-10% (1st year)  | Low commission → rarely sold            | ✅ **THIS** (pure protection) |
| **ULIP**              | 40-60% (1st year) | High commission → aggressively sold     | ❌ Avoid (high charges)       |
| **Endowment Plan**    | 25-35% (1st year) | Medium-high commission                  | ❌ Avoid (4-6% returns)       |
| **Money-Back Policy** | 30-40% (1st year) | High commission, "regular income" pitch | ❌ Avoid (returns <5%)        |

**Lesson:** Agent recommendation ≠ your best interest (follow the money)

### The Nudge (Choice Architecture)

**Example 1: Default Option Bias**

- **Scenario:** Health insurance form pre-checks "Room rent upgrade (+₹5,000/year)"
- **Result:** 80% keep default (even if unnecessary) - nudged into higher premium

**Example 2: Opt-in vs Opt-out**

- **Organ Donation:** Opt-in countries (India) = 20% donors, Opt-out countries (Spain) = 95% donors
- **Insurance:** Auto-enrollment in employer health plan (good nudge)

**Takeaway:** Be aware of defaults - make conscious choices, not passive acceptance.

### Skin in the Game (Deductibles & Co-pay)

```
Moral Hazard: Over-consumption when risk is insured (free healthcare → unnecessary tests)

Solution: Deductibles & Co-pay

Deductible: First ₹X you pay (e.g., ₹25,000 deductible = you pay first ₹25K)
Co-pay: You pay X% of every claim (e.g., 10% co-pay on ₹1L bill = you pay ₹10K)

Example:
- Health plan without deductible: ₹25,000 premium
- Same plan with ₹50,000 deductible: ₹15,000 premium (save ₹10,000)

Best for: Healthy individuals (rare claims) - pocket minor expenses, insure catastrophic
```

### Dunning-Kruger Effect (Overconfidence)

**Stage 1 (Peak of Mt. Stupid):**

- Beginner reads 2 articles → "I know everything about insurance"
- Buys ULIP thinking it's superior (combines insurance + investment!)

**Stage 2 (Valley of Despair):**

- Realizes ULIP returns 4-6% (equity MF gives 12-15%)
- Policy locked for 5 years (high surrender charges)

**Stage 3 (Plateau of Sustainability):**

- Learns: Buy term insurance (₹50L cover for ₹10K/year) + Invest in MF/equity (₹40K/year)
- Total cost same, but death benefit = ₹50L (vs ₹10L in ULIP) + better investment returns

**Lesson:** Question assumptions, seek independent advice (not from sellers).

## 💡 Practical Applications

### For Individuals

**Recommended Insurance Portfolio (Age 30, married, 1 child):**

| Insurance                   | Coverage               | Annual Premium   | Why                           |
| --------------------------- | ---------------------- | ---------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Term Life**               | ₹1 Cr (20-year)        | ₹12,000          | Income replacement for family |
| **Health (Family Floater)** | ₹10L (4 members)       | ₹20,000          | Medical emergencies           |
| **Health (Super Top-up)**   | ₹50L (deductible ₹10L) | ₹8,000           | Catastrophic illness          |
| **Personal Accident**       | ₹50L                   | ₹1,500           | Disability/accidental death   |
| **Total**                   | -                      | **₹41,500/year** | Pure protection               |

**What NOT to Buy:**

- ❌ LIC Endowment (₹50,000/year for 25 years = ₹12.5L invested, ₹20L maturity @ 25 years = 4.1% IRR)
- ❌ ULIP (5-year lock-in, 3-5% charges, poor fund performance)
- ❌ Money-back policy (5-6% returns, partial withdrawals = reduced cover)

**Alternative (Term + MF):**

- Term insurance: ₹12,000/year (₹1 Cr cover)
- Mutual fund SIP: ₹38,000/year (assuming ₹50,000 budget)
- Death benefit: ₹1 Cr (vs ₹20L in endowment)
- Investment corpus (25 years @ 12%): ₹60L (vs ₹20L in endowment)

## 📊 Quick Reference

### Term Insurance vs Traditional Plans

| Feature                    | Term Insurance        | Endowment     | ULIP                     |
| -------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Death Benefit**          | ₹1 Cr                 | ₹20L          | ₹15L                     |
| **Premium (₹/year)**       | ₹12,000               | ₹50,000       | ₹50,000                  |
| **25-Year Cost**           | ₹3L                   | ₹12.5L        | ₹12.5L                   |
| **Maturity Value**         | ₹0 (no survival)      | ₹20L          | ₹18L (if 8% returns)     |
| **IRR (if survive)**       | N/A                   | 4.1%          | 5-7%                     |
| **Investment Flexibility** | Buy separately        | Locked        | 5Y lock-in               |
| **Transparency**           | High (pure insurance) | Low (bundled) | Medium (charges complex) |

**Verdict:** Term insurance + Separate MF (₹38K SIP @ 12% = ₹60L in 25Y) >> Endowment/ULIP

### Health Insurance Optimization

**Sum Insured Calculation:**

```
Base Health Plan = ₹5L - ₹10L (family floater)
Super Top-up = ₹30L - ₹50L (kicks in after base exhausted)

Example:
- Base: ₹10L (premium ₹20,000)
- Top-up: ₹50L (deductible ₹10L, premium ₹8,000)
- Total coverage: ₹60L (₹28,000/year)

Alternative (no top-up):
- Single plan: ₹60L (premium ₹55,000)
- Savings: ₹27,000/year with top-up!
```

### Behavioral Economics Concepts in Insurance

| Concept                | Definition                             | Insurance Example                                        |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Perverse Incentive** | Reward structure → unintended behavior | High commission → agent sells ULIPs (bad for customer)   |
| **Nudge**              | Subtle push toward choice              | Default checkboxes (unnecessary riders)                  |
| **Skin in the Game**   | Personal risk alignment                | Deductibles → fewer frivolous claims                     |
| **Dunning-Kruger**     | Overconfidence in ignorance            | "I understand insurance" after 1 article                 |
| **Loss Aversion**      | Fear of loss >desire for gain          | Buying return-of-premium (paying extra for premium back) |
| **Anchoring**          | First number influences perception     | Agent shows ₹1L plan, then ₹50K looks cheap              |

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. **Buying from relatives/friends:** Emotional pressure → bad products (ULIPs)
2. **Mixing insurance + investment:** Endowment plans (4-6% returns vs 12% in MF)
3. **Under-insuring life:** ₹5L cover when annual income is ₹10L (need 10-15x)
4. **Over-insuring health:** ₹1 Cr cover at age 25 (unnecessary, expensive)
5. **Ignoring claim settlement ratio:** Buying cheapest without checking CSR (delays)
6. **Not reading exclusions:** "Pre-existing disease covered after 4 years" (fine print)
7. **Buying child plans:** Locked for 18 years, poor returns (better: term + MF for child)
8. **Return-of-premium term:** Paying 40% extra to get premium back (fails time-value test)
9. **Multiple small policies:** 5 policies of ₹10L each (better: 1 policy of ₹50L)
10. **Not reviewing annually:** Life changes (marriage, child) → coverage needs change
