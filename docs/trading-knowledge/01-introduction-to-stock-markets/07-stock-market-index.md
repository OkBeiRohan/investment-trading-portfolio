## 7.1 – Overview

An index is a statistical indicator that represents the overall performance of a group of stocks. Think of it as a temperature gauge for the stock market.

## 7.2 – India's Key Indices

**Sensex (BSE Sensitive Index)**

- Consists of 30 well-established companies
- Base year: 1978-79 (Base value: 100)
- Oldest index in India
- Managed by BSE

**Nifty 50 (NSE Fifty)**

- Consists of 50 large-cap companies
- Base year: 1995 (Base value: 1000)
- Represents ~65% of NSE's market cap
- Most traded index

## 7.3 – Practical uses of the Index

1. **Market Barometer** – Tells you if market is up or down
2. **Benchmarking** – Compare your portfolio returns against index
3. **Index Funds** – Invest in the index itself
4. **Derivatives Trading** – Trade Nifty/Sensex futures and options

## 7.4 – Index Construction

Indices use **Free-Float Market Capitalization** method.

**Market Cap** = Number of Shares × Share Price

**Free-Float Market Cap** = Free-float Shares × Share Price

Free-float excludes:

- Promoter holdings
- Government holdings
- Locked-in shares

**Example: Nifty 50 Weight Calculation**

If Company A has:

- Total shares: 100 crores
- Free-float: 40 crores (60% with promoters)
- Share price: Rs. 500

Free-float Market Cap = 40 crores × Rs. 500 = Rs. 20,000 crores

This market cap determines the stock's weight in the index.

## 7.5 – Sector-specific indices

Apart from broad indices, there are sector indices:

- **Nifty Bank** – Banking stocks
- **Nifty IT** – IT sector stocks
- **Nifty Pharma** – Pharmaceutical stocks
- **Nifty Auto** – Automobile stocks
- **Nifty FMCG** – Fast-moving consumer goods

These help track sector-specific performance.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Indices represent overall market/sector performance
2. Sensex has 30 stocks, Nifty has 50 stocks
3. Indices use free-float market capitalization method
4. Useful for benchmarking portfolio performance
5. Sector indices track specific industry segments
6. Can invest in indices through index funds/ETFs
