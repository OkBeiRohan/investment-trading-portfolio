# Clearing and Settlement Process

> **Understanding the post-trade process and T+2 settlement cycle**

**Module:** Introduction to Stock Markets  
**Chapter:** 10 of 15  
**Source:** [Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/varsity/chapter/clearing-and-settlement-process/)  
**Reading Time:** ~10 minutes

---

## 10.1 – Overview

When you buy or sell shares, the actual exchange of securities and money doesn't happen immediately. There's a systematic process called "clearing and settlement."

## 10.2 – The T+2 Settlement Cycle

India follows a **T+2 settlement cycle**, where:

- **T** = Trade Day (day you buy/sell)
- **T+1** = One day after trade
- **T+2** = Two days after trade (settlement day)

**Example:**

- Monday (T): You buy 100 shares of Reliance
- Tuesday (T+1): Processing
- Wednesday (T+2): Shares credited to your demat account

## 10.3 – The Settlement Process

### When you BUY shares:

**T Day** – You place buy order

- Money gets blocked from your trading account
- Order gets executed

**T+1 Day** – Clearing corporation processes

- Verifies all transactions
- Calculates obligations

**T+2 Day** – Settlement

- Shares credited to your demat account
- Money debited from your bank account
- You become official shareholder

### When you SELL shares:

**T Day** – You place sell order

- Shares get blocked in your demat
- Order gets executed

**T+1 Day** – Clearing

- Shares debited from your demat

**T+2 Day** – Settlement

- Money credited to your bank account
- Buyer receives shares

## 10.4 – What is earmarking?

When you sell shares, they're "earmarked" or blocked in your demat account on T day itself. This ensures you don't sell the same shares twice.

Similarly, when you buy shares, the money is blocked to ensure you have sufficient funds.

## 10.5 – Role of Clearing Corporation

**NSCCL (NSE Clearing Corporation)** and **ICCL (BSE Clearing Corporation)** act as central counterparties.

They:

- Guarantee all trades
- Calculate obligations
- Ensure smooth settlement
- Manage risk
- Handle defaults if any

This ensures even if the seller/buyer defaults, you still get your shares/money.

## 10.6 – Pay-in and Pay-out

**Pay-in** – When you transfer shares/money to clearing corporation

**Pay-out** – When clearing corporation transfers shares/money to you

For securities:

- Pay-in: T+2 day (you give shares you sold)
- Pay-out: T+2 day (you receive shares you bought)

For funds:

- Pay-in: T+2 day (you give money for purchase)
- Pay-out: T+2 day (you receive money from sale)

## 10.7 – Auction Settlement

If a seller fails to deliver shares, they go to "auction." The exchange buys shares from market at prevailing price and delivers to buyer. The seller has to bear the extra cost.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. India follows T+2 settlement cycle
2. Shares take 2 days to credit to demat account after purchase
3. Money takes 2 days to credit to bank account after sale
4. Clearing corporations guarantee all trades
5. Earmarking prevents double selling
6. Auction mechanism protects buyers
