## 6.1 – Public Limited Company

After a successful IPO, a company becomes a publicly listed company. Its shares can be freely bought and sold on stock exchanges by anyone.

## 6.2 – What is the stock market?

The stock market is a marketplace where stocks of publicly listed companies are traded. In India, we have two major stock exchanges:

1. **Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE)** – Established in 1875, one of Asia's oldest
2. **National Stock Exchange (NSE)** – Established in 1992, largest in India by volume

##6.3 – What moves the stock?

Stock prices move based on:

- **Demand and Supply** – Fundamental driver
- **Company Performance** – Earnings, growth
- **Economic Factors** – GDP, inflation, interest rates
- **Industry Trends** – Sector-specific developments
- **Market Sentiment** – Investor psychology

## 6.4 – How does the stock get traded?

Trading happens electronically through stock exchanges. When you place an order to buy shares, it gets matched with a seller's order at the exchange.

## 6.5 – What happens after you own stock?

Once you buy shares:

1. They get credited to your Demat account (T+2 days)
2. You become a shareholder of the company
3. You're entitled to dividends (if declared)
4. You can vote in company meetings
5. You can sell anytime during market hours

## 6.6 – A note on the holding period

**Intraday Trading** – Buy and sell on the same day
**Short-term** – Holding for less than 1 year
**Long-term** – Holding for more than 1 year

Holding period affects taxation. Long-term capital gains on equity have different tax treatment than short-term gains.

## 6.7 – How to calculate returns?

**Absolute Returns** = (Selling Price - Purchase Price) / Purchase Price × 100

Example: Buy at Rs. 100, Sell at Rs. 130
Absolute Return = (130 - 100) / 100 × 100 = 30%

**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** – Used for multi-year returns

CAGR = [(Ending Value / Beginning Value)^(1/Years)] - 1

Example: Investment grows from Rs. 10,000 to Rs. 16,000 in 3 years
CAGR = [(16,000 / 10,000)^(1/3)] - 1 = 16.96%

## 6.8 – Where do you fit in?

As an investor, you can:

**Invest** – Buy stocks for long term, based on fundamentals
**Trade** – Buy/sell for short term, based on technical analysis
**Or Both** – Many successful investors do both

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Stock markets facilitate buying/selling of shares
2. Prices move based on demand-supply and multiple factors
3. Trading happens electronically through exchanges
4. Shares take T+2 days to credit to demat account
5. Holding period affects taxation
6. Returns can be measured as absolute returns or CAGR
