## 2.1 – Overview

In the previous chapter, we explored why it is important to save and invest. We also looked at the different asset classes available for investment. We understood that equity as an asset class offered the best risk-reward ratio when invested for the long term. In this chapter, we will understand the Indian stock market structure and learn about its key participants.

## 2.2 – The Indian Stock Market Structure

The stock market in India functions under strict regulat

ions and is monitored by several regulatory bodies. At the top of the hierarchy sits the Ministry of Finance and the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. These ministries formulate various policies and legislations to govern the market participants and the markets themselves.

Under the ministries, there are two regulatory bodies:

1. **Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** – Regulates the securities markets in India
2. **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** – India's central bank that regulates the monetary policy and banking in India

These regulatory bodies establish specific policies to regulate participants operating in the Indian financial ecosystem. The participants include stock exchanges, depositories, brokers, mutual funds, foreign portfolio investors, and other capital market participants.

## 2.3 – The Regulator

**The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)** is the regulatory body for the securities market in India. It was first established in 1988 as a non-statutory body. It was given statutory powers in 1992 through the SEBI Act, 1992. After the Harshad Mehta scam in 1992, SEBI was given additional powers.

SEBI has four main functions:

1. **Protect the interest of investors** – SEBI's primary role is to protect the interest of retail investors
2. **Promote the development of the market** – SEBI works towards developing the securities market through various means
3. **Regulate the securities market** – SEBI formulates rules and regulations which govern the market
4. **Establish a code of conduct for intermediaries** – SEBI lays down rules and a code of conduct for market intermediaries like brokers, sub-brokers, etc.

Some important regulations introduced by SEBI include:

- **SEBI (Stock Brokers and Sub-Brokers) Regulations, 1992** – Governs stockbrokers and sub-brokers
- **SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations, 1992** – Regulates merchant banking activities
- **SEBI (Mutual Funds) Regulations, 1996** – Governs mutual funds
- **SEBI (Buyback of Securities) Regulations, 1998** – Regulates share buyback programs
- **SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015** – Mandates disclosure norms for listed companies
- **SEBI (Prohibition of Insider Trading) Regulations, 2015** – Prevents insider trading
- **SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018** – Governs capital issuance

SEBI continuously monitors market participants and takes action against any violations. It has the power to investigate, penalize, and even ban entities from operating in the markets.

## 2.4 – The Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

The Reserve Bank of India is India's central bank. It was established on April 1, 1935, in accordance with the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. Though initially privately owned, RBI has been fully owned by the Government of India since 1949.

RBI's primary functions include:

1. **Monetary Authority** – Formulates and implements monetary policy
2. **Regulator of the Banking System** – Regulates and supervises banks
3. **Manager of Foreign Exchange** – Manages the foreign exchange market in India
4. **Issuer of Currency** – Has the sole right to issue currency notes in India
5. **Banker to the Government** – Acts as banker to both Central and State Governments
6. **Regulator of Payment Systems** – Regulates payment and settlement systems

While SEBI regulates securities markets, RBI regulates banking and payment systems. Together, they ensure the smooth functioning of India's financial ecosystem.

## 2.5 – Market Participants

Apart from regulators, there are several other entities that participate in the stock market ecosystem:

1. **Stock Exchanges** – Platforms where securities are bought and sold (BSE, NSE)
2. **Stockbrokers** – Entities that facilitate buying and selling of securities
3. **Depositories** – Hold securities in electronic form (NSDL, CDSL)
4. **Depository Participants (DP)** – Act as intermediaries between depositories and investors
5. **Banks** – Facilitate fund transfers and payments
6. **Portfolio Managers** – Manage investment portfolios on behalf of clients
7. **Mutual Funds** – Pool money from investors and invest in securities
8. **Insurance Companies** – Invest insurance premiums in securities
9. **Foreign Portfolio Investors (FPI)** – Foreign entities investing in Indian securities
10. **Credit Rating Agencies** – Rate the creditworthiness of companies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE)
11. **Registrar and Transfer Agents** – Maintain records of shareholders
12. **Clearing Corporations** – Clear and settle trades

Each of these participants plays a crucial role in ensuring the stock market functions smoothly and efficiently.

## 2.6 – Key Regulatory Actions

SEBI has taken several landmark actions over the years to protect investor interests:

1. **Harshad Mehta Scam (1992)** – Led to strengthening of SEBI powers
2. **Ketan Parekh Scam (2000-2001)** – Led to stricter broker regulations
3. **Satyam Computer Services Fraud (2009)** – Led to stricter corporate governance norms
4. **IPO Reforms** – Introduction of book building process, mandatory disclosures
5. **ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount)** – Simplified IPO application process
6. **Algorithmic Trading Regulations** – Introduced framework for algo trading
7. **Commodity Derivatives** – Brought commodity derivatives under SEBI regulation

---

## Key Takeaways

1. SEBI is the primary regulator for securities markets in India
2. RBI regulates banking and payment systems
3. SEBI's main objectives are to protect investors, promote market development, and regulate market participants
4. The stock market ecosystem consists of multiple participants including exchanges, brokers, depositories, and other intermediaries
5. Regulators continuously update rules to ensure fair and transparent markets
6. Historical scams have led to strengthening of regulatory frameworks
