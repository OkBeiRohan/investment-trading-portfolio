## 5.1 – IPO Timeline

The IPO process is a well-structured journey that a company undertakes. Let's understand the complete timeline.

## 5.2 – The IPO Application Process

When a company announces an IPO, retail investors can apply through various channels:

1. **Through your broker's platform** – Most convenient method
2. **Through bank's net banking** – If bank is ASBA enabled
3. **Through Registrar's website** – Company's registrar handles applications

**ASBA (Application Supported by Blocked Amount)** is the standard process where funds are blocked in your account until allotment.

## 5.3 – Price Band and Bidding

IPOs have a price band, for example Rs. 350 to Rs. 380. You can bid at any price within this range. The final price is determined through book building.

## 5.4 – Subscription and Over subscription

When demand exceeds supply, the IPO is said to be oversubscribed. For example:

- Shares offered: 10,00,000
- Applications received for: 1,00,00,000 shares
- Oversubscription: 10 times

## 5.5 – Closure

After the IPO closes, the company along with merchant bankers analyzes bids and determines the final issue price. This is called "price discovery."

## 5.6 – Recent IPOs in India

Notable IPOs include companies like Zomato, Nykaa, Paytm, CarTrade, Policybazaar, and others. Each had different levels of subscription and listing gains.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. Apply for IPOs through ASBA process
2. IPOs have a price band for bidding
3. Oversubscription indicates high demand
4. Allotment is done proportionately or through lottery
5. Shares get listed on stock exchange after allotment
6. Listing price can be higher or lower than issue price
