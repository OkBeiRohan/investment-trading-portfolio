## 📖 Overview

This comprehensive module expands your trading universe beyond equity markets into currencies, commodities, and sovereign debt instruments. You'll learn to trade forex pairs (USD/INR, EUR/INR), commodities (gold, silver, crude oil, metals, agri), and invest in G-Secs (government bonds) for fixed income.

Currency markets are influenced by macroeconomic factors like interest rate differentials, trade balances, and geopolitical events. Commodity markets have unique drivers - seasonal demand (cardamom), geopolitical supply shocks (crude oil), and industrial demand (copper, aluminum). G-Secs provide a risk-free benchmark for portfolio construction.

The module covers contract specifications, pricing factors, hedging applications (importers/exporters), and recent additions like electricity derivatives. You'll understand how interest rate parity dictates forex pricing, how crude oil futures are settled, and how to trade commodity options for defined-risk strategies.

| **JPYINR** | ¥100,000 | ₹0.0025   | ~3-4%          | Last working day of month |

### Interest Rate Parity (IRP)

```
Forward Rate = Spot Rate × (1 + r_domestic × t) / (1 + r_foreign × t)

Example (USD/INR):
Spot = ₹83.00
India rate = 6.5% per annum
US rate = 4.5% per annum
Time to expiry = 90 days (0.25 years)

Fair Value = 83.00 × (1 + 0.065 × 0.25) / (1 + 0.045 × 0.25)
           = 83.00 × 1.01625 / 1.01125
           = 83.41

If trading @ 83.60 → Overpriced (sell futures, buy spot - cash-and-carry arbitrage)
```

### Gold Pricing Factors

**Inverse Relationships:**

- USD Index ↑ → Gold ↓ (priced in USD)
- Real Interest Rates ↑ → Gold ↓ (opportunity cost)

**Positive Relationships:**

- Inflation ↑ → Gold ↑ (hedge)
- Geopolitical Risk ↑ → Gold ↑ (safe haven)
- Central Bank Buying ↑ → Gold ↑ (demand)

### Government Securities Basics

**Types:**

- **T-Bills:** <1 year (91-day, 182-day, 364-day) - Zero-coupon
- **Dated G-Secs:** 2-40 years - Coupon-bearing (semi-annual)
- **SDLs:** State Development Loans (state govt debt)

**Yield Curve:**

```
Normal: Short-term < Long-term (e.g., 6% → 7% → 7.5%)
Inverted: Short-term > Long-term (recession signal)
Flat: All maturities similar (uncertainty)
```

## 💡 Practical Applications

### For Traders

**USD/INR Trade (Expecting rupee depreciation):**

1. **Setup:** Import bill due in 3 months, USD @ ₹83
2. **Strategy:** Buy 10 lots USDINR futures @ ₹83.20 (locks rate)
3. **Lot Value:** $1,000 × 10 × ₹83.20 = ₹8,32,000
4. **Margin:** ₹25,000 (3%)
5. **Outcome:** If spot reaches ₹85, futures profit = (85 - 83.20) × 10,000 = ₹18,000 offsets import cost increase

**Gold Options (Limited-risk speculation):**

1. **Setup:** Expecting volatility (Fed meeting, war risk)
2. **Strategy:** Buy ATM gold call option (₹62,000 strike)
3. **Cost:** ₹500/10g × 100g = ₹5,000 (max loss)
4. **Breakeven:** ₹62,500
5. **Profit:** If gold rallies to ₹65,000 → (65,000 - 62,500) × 10 = ₹25,000 profit

### For Investors

**G-Sec Ladder (₹10L to invest):**

1. **1-year T-Bill:** ₹2L @ 6.5% → Safe liquidity
2. **3-year G-Sec:** ₹3L @ 7.0% → Medium-term
3. **10-year G-Sec:** ₹5L @ 7.3% → Long-term, tradable
4. **Benefits:** Staggered maturities, rate risk diversification, sovereign credit

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes

1. **Ignoring currency correlation:** Trading USD/INR without watching DXY (Dollar Index)
2. **Not factoring storage costs:** Commodities have carry costs (unlike equities)
3. **Trading illiquid contracts:** Far-month commodity futures have wide spreads
4. **Ignoring expiry calendars:** MCX expiries differ from NSE equity derivatives
5. **Not hedging commodity risk:** Exporters not locking forex rates (unhedged exposure)
6. **Confusing gold spot vs futures:** MCX gold = futures (not physical delivery for retail)
7. **Overleveraging commodities:** Higher volatility = need bigger margin buffer
8. **Ignoring macroeconomic data:** Trading crude without watching EIA inventory reports
9. **Not understanding G-Sec pricing:** Yield ↑ → Price ↓ (inverse relationship)
10. **Missing RBI interventions:** RBI defends USD/INR at key levels (83-84 range historically)
